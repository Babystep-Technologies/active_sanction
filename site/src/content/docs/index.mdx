Sync, screen, and read the score:

{/* sample: illustrative -- reaches seven government endpoints and needs a configured store */}

```ruby
ActiveSanction.sync!

hit = ActiveSanction.screen(
  name: "Vladimir Putin", type: :individual, date_of_birth: "1952-10-07"
).first

hit.score                # => 97.3
```

<div class="screening-aid">
  <strong>This library is a screening aid. It is not legal advice, and it is
  not a compliance program.</strong> Verify every hit against the official
  published list before acting on it. A clean result means <em>nothing over the
  threshold in the lists we currently hold</em> — it does not mean <em>not
  sanctioned</em>.
</div>

The gem is complete on its own — every supported list is fetchable, parseable
and screenable locally, forever, with no account and no key. A commercial
hosted service exists, and what it sells is operations rather than capability.
[The gem and the service](/active_sanction/explanation/) says more.

---

Screening compares folded strings, never published ones. This is the one
thing you can try with no data at all:

{/* sample: runnable */}

```ruby
require "active_sanction"

ActiveSanction::Normalizer.call("O'Brien, Seán").value
# => "o brien sean"
```
